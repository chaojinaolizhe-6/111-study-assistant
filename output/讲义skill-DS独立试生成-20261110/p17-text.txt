Answer (English). The closer the intensities of the two beams, the higher the visibility of the fringes.
With I1 and I2, Imax = I1+I2+2√I1I2, Imin = I1+I2−2√I1I2 and V = (Imax−Imin)/(Imax+Imin) =
2√I1I2/(I1 + I2). When I1 = I2, V = 1: the dark fringes are completely black and the bright ones
are brightest, so the centre can be located and the rings counted reliably, and the parallel adjustment
can be judged. If one beam is much weaker, say 9 : 1, then V = 2

being compared: if the total incident power I1 + I2 is held fixed, an equal split maximises the
bright-fringe peak Imax = I1 + I2 + 2√I1I2 and minimises the dark minimum, hence maximises the
visibility; the period-averaged total intensity, however, remains I1 + I2 and does not increase on equal
splitting. If one instead asks how much power a particular output port receives, the answer depends on
that port’s splitting ratio and cannot be inferred from “equal energies” alone. For fringe observation,
what equal splitting really provides is higher contrast, not a larger average brightness.
