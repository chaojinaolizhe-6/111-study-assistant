Kinematics of a Rigid Body
Summary and exercises
Summary
The velocity of an arbitrary point of a rigid body is given by
v = V + Ω× r
where V is the velocity of an arbitrarily chosen origin of the moving coordinate system (MCS), also referred
to as the (instantaneous) velocity of translational motion; Ωis the (instantaneous) angular velocity; and r
is the radius vector from the origin of the MCS to this point of the rigid body.
If the origin of the MCS is shifted by an arbitrary vector a, the velocity of translational motion changes
according to the formula V′ = V + Ω× a, while the angular velocity remains unchanged.
If, at a given instant, the velocity of translational motion and the angular velocity are orthogonal to each
other, i.e., V · Ω= 0 (this condition is independent of the choice of the MCS origin), then there exists
an origin of the MCS for which its velocity (and hence the velocity of translational motion) becomes zero.
Furthermore, all points of the rigid body located on the axis passing through this point and parallel to the
instantaneous angular velocity Ωare also instantaneously at rest. This axis is called the instantaneous axis
of rotation or instantaneous center of velocities for planar motion. In this case, the entire motion of the
rigid body is an (instantaneous) rotation about the instantaneous axis of rotation.
