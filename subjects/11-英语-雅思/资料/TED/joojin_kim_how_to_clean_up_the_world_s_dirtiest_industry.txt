For those of us who are worried about climate change, it's no doubt challenging times. It feels like the end of the light of the tunnel gets further as companies and governments pull back. And I get it. It's hard to feel motivated with headlines like these. But that is not the full story.

In reality, a shift towards a climate solution-based economy is already underway. Not just because it protects the climate, but because it's becoming more profitable, more practical and more affordable. Despite all the setbacks, we will solve this problem. However, we have to be strategic. We cannot solve every climate problem at once. We have to identify the systems that can unlock many others. The places where one smart move can create ripple effects across the entire economy.

One of the most strategic, most powerful, but also most overlooked of those systems is shipping. If shipping was a country, it would be the sixth largest greenhouse gas emitter in the world. Many of these ships aren't just powered by fossil fuels, they're built with fossil fuels, and they transport fossil fuels.

Maritime shipping is a climate problem on three fronts. One, for the cargo it carries. Crude oil and gas account for roughly one third of maritime trade. Around half of natural gas is shipped in a liquefied form called LNG, and LNG trade has doubled during the past decade. Two, because of the fuel ships burn. Most ships still burn heavy fuel oils or increasingly, LNG, which releases carbon dioxide and methane, a dirty superpollutant that accelerates climate change and air pollution. Three, because of the steel ships are made of. Most steel is made using dirty coal. Steel production is responsible for another 8% of global emissions.

So shipping is not a side issue in the climate crisis. It is one of the biggest hidden levers in the entire climate fight, a system if pulled the right way, which can unlock a faster energy transition.

It's tempting to think of oceans as diffused and decentralized, but when it comes to ships, their supply chains converge in one place: East Asia. South Korea in particular dominates global shipbuilding, steel production for ships and the public finance behind them. And we are now at a critical moment, with Asia as a key leverage point. The world has experienced how volatile fossil fuels are with the war in Ukraine. Again, we are experiencing the same with the war in the Middle East. Right now, East Asian economies are making a decision. Do we keep funding fossil fuels or pivot towards offshore wind installation vessels, green ammonia tankers or ships made with clean steel? Because clean options don't scale just because they exist, they scale when policy, finance and regulation align to make clean attractive.

My organization, Solutions for Our Climate, works to do just that. And we're no beginner here. We've done it before with coal. From behind-the-scenes international negotiations to grassroots communities, from courtrooms to hilarious videos portraying dismal climate facts, we know what message to put in front of who, how and when. Our work actually contributed to ending coal financing by the East Asian coal giants.

(Applause)

And now we're ready to take on shipping the same way. First, we expose emissions, policy gaps, financial risks. Take LNG transportation. Global LNG trade depends entirely on ships. Each LNG carrier ship costs 250 million dollars, takes three years to build and locks in decades of emissions. The system is surprisingly concentrated. 70 percent of these ships are built in one country, South Korea. In 2022 alone, in one year, South Korean government banks provided more than 10 billion dollars of financing for these ships. The technologies around these ships are monopolized by a handful of companies. So this isn't just a business story. It's a political and financial choke point.

Second, we redirect capital and governments away from fossil expansion. If we cut the financing of these ships, we will limit LNG expansion. Our aim is simple. Make LNG a no-investment zone.

(Applause)

We're asking private banks to end the financing of these ships. We're pushing Asian lenders to adopt exclusion policies. Third, we build markets, demands and rules for clean alternatives. The technology for clean steel or green shipping fuels is already here. What's missing is the supply chains and demand to meet it. Our push for finance, regulations and infrastructure will change this. That means building clean fuel supply chains, creating new demand through green shipping corridors between ports. Over time, renewable-rich regions will replace oil refineries as shipping fuel production hubs. We apply the same approach for clean steel. Green hydrogen will replace the role coal served in steelmaking.

This isn't just aspirational. The work is already underway. We've already convinced four European banks to exit from a major LNG project in Mozambique. We've blocked new coal-based steel investments. We've secured financial support for hydrogen-based steel mills. We're doing the work and building the momentum.

And now we will supercharge it through Navigate to Clean, a new initiative to clean up maritime shipping. With the generous support of the community of Audacious donors, we will turn shipping from a conduit of dirty trade to a driver of clean trade. We are a team of marketers, diplomats, lawyers, shipping experts. We're a regional organization with global reach. We know the system and how to move it. We've done it with coal and we can do it with shipping. It is difficult times, yes, but with the right strategy and leverage, we can eliminate up to 2.2 gigatons of emissions per year by 2032. That's almost one year of emissions of the entire European Union.

(Applause)

This is not a marginal fix. This is not tinkering at the edges. This is what it looks like to move a hidden engine of the global economy and the climate movement with it. We're playing chess, not checkers, and we're here to win.

Thank you.

(Applause)
